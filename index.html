Arrows point the way the wind blows, longer for faster wind: black = 10-m wind, dark purple = gusts (10-m direction), blue = transport wind; a dot = under 1 mph. Gray = no forecast data. Wind is the model's 10-m wind (roughly 15–20% above 20-ft wind); gust is WRF's diagnosed surface gust; RH is at 2 m.

Mixing height is WRF's boundary-layer height (ft above ground) and transport wind its mean wind through that layer. Ventilation index = transport wind (kt) × mixing height (ft), in knot-feet. Smoke condition (NWCG PMS 425), knot-feet: Poor 0–28,999 · Marginal 29,000–37,999 · Fair 38,000–49,999 · Good 50,000–94,999 · Excellent 95,000+ (“but burn with caution”: good dispersal can also mean conditions for large fire growth). Some states use other breaks. MPH-FT shows the same index with the wind in mph (×1.15; the breaks become 33k, 44k, 58k and 109k). It is the index of Worsnop et al. 2026 (their Eq. 1, in m²/s; 1 m²/s = 6.38 kt-ft); the bars beside the VI color bar are their burn days' daylight-maximum VI for the month (P pile, B broadcast).

Precipitation is the model's, in inches, hour-ending (the amount that fell in the hour up to the valid time). Day total = local midnight to midnight; the run's first and last days are partial. Run total = since the run started, with the 3 km run's amounts added after hour 72.

Citations and data sources
  • Forecast weather: CANSAC (California and Nevada Smoke and Air Committee) real-time WRF, run by the Desert Research Institute: the 1 km run for the first 72 hours and the 3 km Domain 2 run after that, from CANSAC's GRIB2 files. 10-m U/V wind, WRF surface gust and 2-m relative humidity, regridded to a regular latitude/longitude grid. Where the 1 km grid ends (north of about 42.3°N and east of about 113.4°W) the same hour of the 3 km run fills in.
  • Mixing height, transport wind, ventilation index: WRF planetary boundary layer height (HPBL) and PBL-mean U/V from the same CANSAC files. Ventilation index = mixing height × transport wind speed (Hardy et al. 2001, as Eq. 1 of Worsnop et al. 2026), shown in knot-feet (or mph-ft), computed on the model grid before regridding.
  • Smoke-condition ratings: NWCG, NWCG Guide to Fire Weather Forecasts (PMS 425), Fire Weather Forecast Variables, Table 2. Colors are this viewer's: one hue per rating, shaded within the wider ratings.
  • Worsnop, R.P., A. Hoell, B.J. Hatchett, T.B. Chapman, M.L. Breeden, Z. Tolby, K.C. Short and M. Hobbins. 2026. Characterizing windows of opportunity for prescribed pile and broadcast burning in Northern California. Fire Ecology 22:51. https://doi.org/10.1186/s42408-026-00492-6 — burn-day ventilation index percentiles by month (ONCC, 2011–2024).
  • Hardy, C.C., R.D. Ottmar, J.L. Peterson, J.E. Core and P. Seamon. 2001. Smoke management guide for prescribed and wildland fire, 2001 edition. NWCG PMS 420-2.
  • Color scales: CANSAC's own wind and RH palettes. Wind, gust and transport wind share one scale, 2-mph bands to 40 mph, then wider bands to 100+. Mixing height and VI run dark red (poor dispersal) to blue (good).
  • Boundaries and cities: Natural Earth (public domain). Map areas: NIFC GACC boundaries.

Experimental planning aid: raw model output, not an official forecast. Use the NWS fire weather forecasts and spot forecasts for operations.
